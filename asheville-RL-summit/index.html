Invitational summit

Asheville Summit on Reinforcement Learning in Healthcare

3–5 September 2026Asheville, North Carolina, USA

The Summit brings together leading scientists in reinforcement learning (RL) from methodological, clinical and applied backgrounds. Its goal is to develop actionable solutions to improve the current state of RL in healthcare, and to chart the path from today's offline models to online reinforcement learning embedded in a learning health system.

Guiding questions

  1. What are the unifying semantics, terminology and methods in healthcare RL?
  2. How can offline RL in healthcare deliver more than just another "machine-learned model", and what are the key shortcomings we need to solve in the next five years?
  3. What is the primary barrier, or set of barriers, to achieving an "online" RL evaluation in healthcare, and what must happen to overcome them?
  4. What is the most important next step to achieving the vision of embedded online RL in a learning healthcare system?

Programme

Day 1 — Thursday 3 September

Setting the stage for RL in healthcare

  • Semantics, terminology and finding common ground. A shared vocabulary for RL models in healthcare; defining "offline" and "online"; dynamic treatment regimes and optimal treatment strategies; causal versus RL framings; what "validation" means to each field.
  • All about offline RL for healthcare. What constitutes a state; how to define an action, and whether the data should constrain it; determining rewards, now and in five years.
  • Future offline models. Humans in the model-building loop; ensembles and layered RL for multiple actions; missing data beyond imputation; the optimal offline model in 2030.

Day 2 — Friday 4 September

Moving from offline to online RL

  • Evaluating offline RL policies. Validation and tuning for generalisability; near-causal interpretation of clinical impact; heterogeneity across units and systems; fairness; the role of silent-mode and prospective deployment.
  • Online RL and causality. Defining online RL; multi-armed bandits as a bridge; how online RL resembles a Bayesian adaptive platform trial; whether uncertainty can inform allocation; sequential decisions and heterogeneity of treatment effect.
  • Imagining the ideal online RL system. Design features of a first embedded system; how much learning can be embedded; blending the RCT with embedded RL, including micro-randomisation in specific subsets.
  • Reality check. Data to learn, refine and federate; keeping patients safe while exploring; model updates and their regulation; assembling stakeholder groups; the most efficient path to deployment within two years.

Day 3 — Saturday 5 September

Concrete next steps this year, and by 2035

  • Practical considerations for implementation. Research, clinical care or quality improvement? Opening the conversation with ethics boards; prompt versus mandatory intervention; ideal regulatory oversight; whether early test cases should sit away from the bedside.
  • Synthesis and next steps. Ground covered; written products (white paper, methods, priorities); funding opportunities; a name for the effort.
